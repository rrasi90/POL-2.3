## Research discipline

- **Observed / documented:** directly supported by a source.
- **Company-reported:** claim made by the company.
- **Third-party reported:** reputable external source.
- **Inference:** analytical interpretation.
- **Open:** insufficient evidence.

## High-value hypotheses

### H1 — Existing physical infrastructure can be a competitive asset
Asset-light platforms can treat wholesalers, distributors and logistics providers as infrastructure instead of trying to replace them.

### H2 — Phygital is likely a cold-start mechanism
Physical onboarding appears repeatedly in benchmark cases, but exact universal figures such as “70–80% digital reorder within 60–90 days” must remain company-specific until each number is independently supported.

### H3 — Transaction density should precede sophisticated data monetization
Commerce creates transaction and behavioral data; that data can later support credit, advertising, trade marketing and manufacturer intelligence.

### H4 — 1P inventory/logistics deserves explicit downside modelling
Multiple failures/restructurings support a strong cautionary hypothesis, but failure usually has several interacting causes. “Asset-heavy = failure” is not a universal law.

### H5 — Benchmark status must be time-indexed
Mergers, pivots and insolvency events materially change the comparison set. A benchmark is incomplete without a dated current-status field.

## Audit priorities

1. Verify every major scale/funding/valuation/profitability figure.
2. Verify every current-status claim as of 2026-09-02.
3. Separate company-reported claims from independent evidence.
4. Remove unsupported universal operating metrics.
5. Audit Cartona, OmniRetail, Bazaar, GrowSari and other Tier-1 revenue models.
6. Investigate failure causes independently.
7. Only then propagate conclusions into POL's permanent strategy documents.

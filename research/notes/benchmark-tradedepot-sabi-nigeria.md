# Comparative Benchmark: TradeDepot vs Sabi (Nigeria)

## 1. TradeDepot (Credit-Led Distribution)
- **Founded:** 2016 by Onyekachi Izukanne, Michael Ukpong, Ruzanna Essayan. Backed by IFC, Novastar, Partech, Arcadia ($120M+ raised).
- **Core Product: ShopTopUp:** Micro-credit-driven B2B commerce. Enabled informal retailers to order FMCG inventory on buy-now-pay-later (BNPL) terms.
- **Operational Reality:** Operated a capital-heavy distribution model with leased warehouses and dedicated van fleets. When macroeconomic headwinds, 100%+ food inflation, and the massive Naira devaluation hit Nigeria in 2023-2024, TradeDepot faced severe credit recovery pressures and had to downsize operations, curb balance-sheet lending, and focus on defensive unit economics.

## 2. Sabi (Offline Broker & Wholesaler Enablement)
- **Founded:** 2020 by Anu Adasolum (CEO) and Ademola Adesina. Backed by CRE Venture Capital, Norrsken22, Comini Capital ($60M+ raised).
- **Core Innovation: Digitizing Existing Middlemen:** Unlike TradeDepot, which tried to replace traditional intermediaries, Sabi built a digital rail that **digitized the informal brokers and wholesalers themselves**.
- **Sabi Trace:** Merchant management platform for wholesale aggregators. Sabi provides digital inventory tracking, invoicing, and logistics matching, taking a small transaction fee without holding physical stock.

## 3. Strategic Takeaways for POL
- **The Peril of Balance-Sheet BNPL in High Inflation:** TradeDepot's struggle highlights the extreme danger of funding retailer credit from venture capital or platform balance sheets during currency depreciation. If the currency devalues 50%, the real value of your loan book is cut in half while your default rates spike.
- **The Sabi Lesson: Embrace the Intermediaries:** Sabi grew faster and with far lower capital burn by treating informal wholesale brokers as users rather than enemies. POL must treat Iranian بنکداران (Banakdaran) as primary platform beneficiaries.

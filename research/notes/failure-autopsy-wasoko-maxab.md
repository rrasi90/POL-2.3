# Failure & Restructuring Autopsy: Wasoko & MaxAB

## 1. Timeline & Corporate History
- **2014-2016:** Daniel Yu founds Sokowatch in Kenya (later rebranded to Wasoko). Belal El-Megharbel founds MaxAB in Egypt (2018).
- **2020-2022 (Hyper-Growth Peak):** Both companies champion the **1P Asset-Heavy Model** (owning/leasing massive central fulfillment centers, direct purchasing of FMCG inventory onto their balance sheet, operating fleets of hundreds of delivery vans, and giving unsecured retailer credit).
  - *Wasoko:* Raises $125M Series B in March 2022 led by Tiger Global and Avenir Growth, valued at **$625 Million**. Expands aggressively into Tanzania, Rwanda, Uganda, Zambia, and opens a lavish innovation hub in Zanzibar.
  - *MaxAB:* Raises $40M pre-Series B in late 2022 (Silver Lake, Beco Capital, 4DX), total funding $100M+. Expands into Morocco.
- **2023 (The Reckoning):** VC funding freezes. Massive currency devaluations in Kenya (Shilling drops 30%+) and Egypt (Pound devalues 60%+). Operating cash burn exceeds $3M-$5M per month per company.
- **December 2023 - August 2024 (The Distressed Merger):** Wasoko and MaxAB announce an "all-stock merger of equals." Investor VNV Global writes down Wasoko's valuation from $625M to $260M (a 58% haircut). Combined entity valued at ~$526M upon closing in August 2024.
- **2024-2025 (Brutal Retrenchment):**
  - Combined company lays off thousands of workers (over 10-20% repeatedly).
  - Wasoko completely exits Uganda, Zambia, and Zanzibar. MaxAB scales back regional Egyptian warehouses.
  - Top management exodus: Mohamed Ben Halim (MaxAB co-founder/COO), Wasoko CFO, CTO, HR heads depart.
  - In **September 2025, Wasoko founder Daniel Yu officially steps down as co-CEO**, leaving Belal El-Megharbel as sole CEO.
- **Current State (2026):** Transitioned away from low-margin 1P physical FMCG distribution, desperately pivoting into fintech services and merchant financing to survive.

## 2. What Failed? (Breakdown of Points of Failure)
1. **Gross Margin vs Operating Cost Inversion:** Branded staple FMCG in East Africa and Egypt yields a gross margin of only 3% to 6%. Operating an end-to-end 1P supply chain (warehouse leases, diesel, truck maintenance, driver salaries, shrinkage/theft, field sales force) cost **8% to 12% of GMV**. Every single order lost money on a fully loaded unit economics basis.
2. **Working Capital Drag & Currency Destruction:** Both companies held millions of dollars of imported FMCG goods in warehouses. When the Egyptian Pound and Kenyan Shilling cratered, the dollar-denominated value of their inventory vanished overnight, creating massive balance-sheet holes.
3. **Premature Multi-Country Expansion:** Wasoko entered 5 countries before achieving positive unit economics in Nairobi. Each new market duplicated fixed administrative and warehouse overhead.
4. **Credit Default Spikes:** Unsecured BNPL credit offered to small dukas resulted in significant non-performing loans when food inflation squeezed consumer purchasing power.

## 3. Root Cause Analysis: Documented vs Inference
- **Documented Causes:** High burn rate, reliance on continuous mega-round VC funding, severe currency depreciation, failure to reach unit profitability in secondary markets.
- **Researcher Inference (The Structural Trap):** The fundamental flaw was believing that tech software multiples (20x revenue) applied to a low-margin physical wholesale and trucking business. By taking inventory onto their own balance sheet, they turned themselves into a traditional FMCG distributor with 10x the corporate overhead.

## 4. Counterfactual: Would Asset-Light Have Saved Them?
**YES.** Cartona (Egypt) and OmniRetail (Nigeria) faced the exact same macroeconomic crises (currency crashes, inflation, funding winter). Yet Cartona is approaching EBITDA profitability and OmniRetail is net profitable. Why? Because Cartona and OmniRetail did not own warehouses, did not own trucks, and did not hold depreciating inventory. An asset-light Wasoko/MaxAB would have had an 80% lower cash burn, zero inventory write-downs, and would have scaled sustainably.

## 5. POL Lessons
- **NEVER BUY TRUCKS OR LEASE MEGA-WAREHOUSES:** Owning logistics assets in a 3-5% gross margin industry is corporate suicide.
- **EXPANSION DISCIPLINE:** Do not expand outside Tehran until the core city unit economics are cash-flow positive.

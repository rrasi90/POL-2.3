# Failure Autopsy: Capiter (Egypt)

## 1. Executive Summary & Timeline
- **Company Name:** Capiter
- **Country:** Egypt
- **Founders:** Mahmoud Nouh (CEO, ex-co-founder of SWVL), Ahmed Nouh (COO)
- **Founded:** July 2020
- **Funding:** Raised $33M Series A in September 2021 led by Quona Capital and MSA Capital.
- **Collapse Date:** September 2022 (Dead within 12 months of raising $33M).
- **Current Status:** Completely defunct, liquidated, website offline.

## 2. Forensic Breakdown of the Collapse
- **Reckless Burn Rate:** Following its $33M raise, Capiter burned through the entire capital reserve in less than 18 months (~$2M/month burn) by:
  - Subsidizing FMCG products up to 5-10% below wholesale purchase price to artificially inflate GMV for a rapid Series B pitch.
  - Opening over 15 leased fulfillment centers and hiring thousands of warehouse workers, drivers, and corporate executives at inflated salaries.
  - Expanding prematurely into non-core categories and preparing international launches into Dubai and Riyadh.
- **The Boardroom Coup (September 2022):** In early September 2022, the Board of Directors issued a public statement abruptly firing CEO Mahmoud Nouh and COO Ahmed Nouh with immediate effect. The board accused the founders of breach of fiduciary duty and fleeing the country, as they refused to attend critical in-person due diligence meetings for an emergency merger.
- **Insolvency & Debt Mountain:** When the founders were ousted, it was discovered that Capiter owed millions of dollars to Egyptian banks, FMCG suppliers, and unpaid staff salaries, with zero cash remaining in company bank accounts. Suppliers seized warehouse inventory, and operations halted immediately.

## 3. Root Cause Analysis
- **Primary Cause:** "Growth at all costs" mentality coupled with total absence of financial controls, unit economic discipline, and transparent corporate governance.
- **The Subsidy Illusion:** Capiter proved that in B2B FMCG, acquiring retailers through subsidized pricing creates **zero customer loyalty**. Retailers ordered from Capiter only because oil and sugar were cheaper than the market; the second Capiter stopped the subsidy or went bust, retailers returned to their traditional wholesalers without missing a beat.

## 4. Strategic Lessons for POL
- **The Vanity of Subsidized GMV:** Never subsidize product prices with investor cash. If a transaction does not produce a positive contribution margin on Day 1, it is fictitious volume.
- **Governance & Financial Transparency:** B2B platforms deal with large gross cash flows. Strict internal controls, audited escrow accounts, and board oversight are non-negotiable.

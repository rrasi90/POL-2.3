# Failure Autopsy: MarketForce / RejaReja (Kenya)

## 1. Executive Summary & Timeline
- **Company Name:** MarketForce (B2B platform **RejaReja**)
- **Founders:** Tesh Mbaabu, Mesongo Sibuti
- **Founded:** 2018 (launched RejaReja in 2020)
- **Funding:** Raised $40M+ (including a $20M Series A in early 2022 led by V8 Capital Partners).
- **The Collapse:** In **April 2024, MarketForce officially shut down RejaReja**, exiting B2B e-commerce entirely after burning through capital and suffering negative unit economics. Founders pivoted to **Chpter** (social commerce AI tool).

## 2. Why RejaReja Failed: Tesh Mbaabu's Candid Autopsy
1. **Extreme Price Elasticity & No Brand Loyalty:** Small shopkeepers in Kenya are hyper-sensitive to price. A difference of 5 Kenyan Shillings ($0.04) on a packet of maize flour or oil caused shopkeepers to switch immediately. RejaReja was forced into perpetual price wars with local wholesalers, wiping out gross margins.
2. **Last-Mile Delivery Unit Inversion:** Delivering low-ticket orders ($20 - $40 AOV) across traffic-choked Nairobi to individual kiosks cost more than the total gross margin of the basket.
3. **Credit Default Trap:** Attempted to drive retention with credit partner Pezesha, leading to debt disputes and uncollectible merchant loans.

## 3. Strategic Lessons for POL
- **Low Price is NOT a Retention Moat:** If retailers use your platform only because you are cheaper than the wholesale market, you do not have Product-Market Fit; you have a subsidy program.
- **The Minimum Viable Basket (AOV):** B2B FMCG delivery cannot survive on tiny order sizes. Platforms must enforce strict Minimum Order Quantities (MOQ) or delivery fees.
